    The search

    Eight rules compete. Each generation, the dullest ones (dead, frozen or pure static) are replaced by mutated offspring of the most complex. Tap any dish to study it up close.

    Generation 0

    One rule, every cell

    A cell measures the average life in three rings around it: near, middle and far. Each ring feeds a bell curve that says "grow" when the reading is just right and "shrink" otherwise. The weighted sum decides the cell's next value.

    Structure at every scale

    Because cells sense at several distances at once, small structures sit inside larger ones: spots inside worms, worms inside colonies, membranes around it all. No level is programmed. Each emerges from the one below.

    How the search judges

    The complexity score is a simple heuristic. It rewards patterns that keep moving, have structure at more than one scale, and neither die out nor boil into noise. It's a small evolutionary search, not a trained model.

    Built on ideas from Lenia (Bert Wang-Chak Chan) and neural cellular automata (Mordvintsev et al., 2020). The rule is a continuous multi-kernel automaton, essentially a one-layer network that every cell shares. It runs on your GPU.
